# Genre Notes

Short listening notes for every genre in `songs.csv`. VibeMatch retrieves the notes for the genres it recommends and gives them to the model, so its explanation can say why a genre fits an activity without making things up. Written by me for this project.

## pop
Catchy, vocal-led songs with a steady beat. Good for driving, cleaning, or a warm-up. High-energy pop works for the gym; mid-tempo pop is easy background for friends.

## indie pop
Pop with lighter production and more guitar. Upbeat but less polished than mainstream pop. Good for walks, road trips and getting ready to go out.

## lofi
Slow, looping beats with soft piano or guitar and little or no singing. Made for studying, coding and reading because it stays out of the way. Low energy and very steady.

## ambient
Long, slow textures with almost no beat. Used for sleep, meditation and deep focus. The lowest-energy genre in the catalog after classical.

## classical
Instrumental pieces, often piano or strings. Calm pieces help with reading and winding down; there are no lyrics to distract from words on a page.

## jazz
Acoustic instruments, swing rhythm and improvisation. Relaxed jazz fits coffee shops, cooking and quiet evenings.

## rock
Electric guitars, live drums and strong vocals. Intense rock is for workouts, running and blowing off steam. Too loud for studying for most people.

## metal
The heaviest and fastest genre in the catalog, with distorted guitars and aggressive vocals. Used for hard training sessions or releasing anger. Often explicit.

## edm
Electronic dance music built for clubs, with drops and a fast four-on-the-floor beat. Very high energy. Good for running, cardio and parties.

## synthwave
Retro 1980s-style synthesizers and drum machines. Moody and nostalgic, with a mid-to-high tempo. Popular for night drives and late coding sessions.

## hip hop
Rap vocals over strong beats. Confident hip hop works for workouts and getting hyped. Lyrics are front and center, so it is distracting for reading, and it is often explicit.

## r&b
Smooth vocals, slower grooves and romantic themes. Good for dinners, slow evenings and dates.

## country
Story-driven songs with acoustic guitar and twang. Nostalgic and easygoing. Good for road trips and relaxed afternoons.

## folk
Acoustic, quiet and personal, often one voice and a guitar. Melancholy folk suits rainy days and reflection.

## reggae
Laid-back off-beat rhythm and warm bass. Relaxed and sunny. Good for the beach, cooking and slow weekends.

## latin
Rhythms like reggaeton, salsa and cumbia. Playful and very danceable. Good for parties, dancing and upbeat workouts.

## blues
Guitar and voice built on sad, slow progressions. Good for late nights and reflective moods. Rarely used for workouts.
